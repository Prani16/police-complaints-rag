NHRC Guidelines on Intimation and Reporting of Custodial Deaths

Summary notes compiled from National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) instructions and press releases.

1. The 1993 instruction: intimation within 24 hours

On 14 December 1993 the NHRC issued instructions (letter No. 66/SG/NHRC/93) to the Chief Secretaries of all States and Union Territories. District Magistrates and Superintendents of Police of every district must report every custodial death and custodial rape to the Commission within 24 hours of its occurrence. The Commission warned that failure to report promptly would give rise to a presumption that there was an attempt to suppress the incident.

The intimation may be sent by fax, email or telegram. The 24-hour intimation must later be followed by the post-mortem report, the videography of the post-mortem, and the report of the magisterial inquest.

2. The 2001 guidelines: complete reports within two months

Reports were frequently delayed. For example, in 1999-2000 full reports were still awaited in 165 of 177 cases of death in police custody and in 520 of 916 cases of death in judicial custody. To streamline the procedure, the Commission issued fresh guidelines on 5 January 2001, signed by its Secretary General, to the Home Secretaries, Directors General of Prisons and Directors General of Police of all States and Union Territories.

  • Deadline: All reports, including the post-mortem report, videography and the magisterial inquiry report, must reach the Commission within two months of the incident.
  • Post-mortem format: The post-mortem must be conducted using the model proforma designed by the Commission.
  • Magisterial inquiry: A magisterial inquiry must be held in every case of custodial death and completed as early as possible, within the two-month deadline.
  • Viscera report: The post-mortem report and other documents must not be held back while waiting for the viscera examination; the viscera report is sent separately once it is received.

3. Deaths in police encounters

Revised guidelines issued on 2 December 2003 require States to send intimation of all deaths arising out of police encounters as well. Where a complaint is made against the police alleging a criminal act that makes out a cognizable case of culpable homicide, an FIR must be registered. For the Union Territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh, the Commission asked for intimation of custodial deaths within 24 hours and of encounter deaths within 48 hours.

4. Magisterial enquiry guidelines (2023)

On 27 January 2023 the NHRC published guidelines on conducting magisterial enquiries in cases of death in custody or in the course of police action. Under the BNSS, 2023 the magistrate's inquiry into a custodial death (earlier Section 176(1A) CrPC) now falls under Section 196 BNSS.

5. Enforcement in practice

The Commission continues to take suo motu cognizance where intimation is not sent. In May 2025 it took suo motu cognizance of a reported death of a man in police custody in Deoghar district, Jharkhand, and sought an explanation from the Chief Secretary and the Director General of Police because the district police had not sent intimation within 24 hours as its guidelines require.

Frequently asked questions

Who must report a custodial death to the NHRC?
The District Magistrate and Superintendent of Police of the district (and prison officials for deaths in judicial custody).
What happens if a death is not reported?
The Commission may presume an attempt to suppress the incident, take suo motu cognizance, and call for explanations from senior State officials.
