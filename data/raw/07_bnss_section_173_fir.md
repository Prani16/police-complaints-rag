# Section 173 BNSS, 2023 – Information in Cognizable Cases (FIR)

> Summary notes. The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023 replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 with effect from **1 July 2024**. Section 173 BNSS corresponds to Section 154 CrPC.

## 173(1) – Giving information about a cognizable offence

- Information about the commission of a cognizable offence can be given to the **officer in charge of a police station**, either **orally** or by **electronic communication**.
- It can be given **irrespective of the area where the offence was committed**. Questions of territorial jurisdiction are dealt with later, during investigation and trial. This is the statutory basis of the **Zero FIR**.
- **Oral information:** the officer (or a person under the officer's direction) reduces it to writing, reads it over to the informant, and gets it signed by the informant. The substance is entered in a book kept in the form prescribed by the State government.
- **Electronic information (e-FIR):** the information is taken on record once it is **signed within three days** by the person giving it.

### Safeguards for women informants

When a woman gives information about specified offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), including attempts, the information must be **recorded by a woman police officer**.

### Safeguards for persons with disabilities

- Where the person against whom such an offence is alleged has a temporary or permanent mental or physical disability, the information is recorded at the person's **residence or another convenient place of their choice**.
- An **interpreter or special educator** must be present where needed, and the recording must be **videographed**.
- The police officer must arrange to have the person's statement recorded by a **Magistrate under Section 183** as soon as possible.

## 173(2) – Free copy

A copy of the recorded information must be given **immediately and free of cost** to the **informant or the victim**.

## 173(3) – Preliminary enquiry

| Condition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Offences covered | Cognizable offences punishable with **3 years or more but less than 7 years** |
| Permission | Prior permission of an officer **not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police (DSP)** |
| Time limit | Must be completed within **14 days** |
| Purpose | To ascertain whether a **prima facie case** exists for proceeding |
| Mandatory? | No – the officer decides based on the nature and gravity of the offence |

## 173(4) – Remedy when the police refuse to record information

1. The aggrieved person may send the **substance of the information in writing, by post, to the Superintendent of Police (SP)**.
2. If the SP is satisfied that a cognizable offence is disclosed, the SP investigates personally or directs a subordinate officer to investigate. That officer has all the powers of an officer in charge of a police station for that offence.
3. If relief is still not obtained, the person may **apply to the Magistrate**.

## Key differences from Section 154 CrPC

| Topic | CrPC Section 154 | BNSS Section 173 |
|---|---|---|
| Electronic reporting | Not expressly recognised | Expressly allowed; signature within 3 days |
| Territorial jurisdiction | Zero FIR developed through court decisions and practice | Expressly allowed "irrespective of the area" |
| Copy of FIR | Given to the informant | Given to the informant **or victim**, immediately and free |
| Preliminary enquiry | Only through judicial decisions (Lalita Kumari) in limited categories | Statutory, 3–7 year offences, DSP permission, 14 days |
| Refusal remedy | SP and Magistrate | Same, set out in an express sub-section (4) |

## Related provisions

- **Section 174 BNSS** (earlier Section 155 CrPC): information about **non-cognizable** offences. The officer records the substance in the prescribed book and refers the informant to the Magistrate.
- **Section 175 BNSS**: police officer's power to investigate cognizable cases.
- **Section 183 BNSS**: recording of confessions and statements by a Magistrate.
