D.K. Basu v. State of West Bengal: the 11 arrest and detention guidelines

Citation: AIR 1997 SC 610; (1997) 1 SCC 416. Court: Supreme Court of India. Bench: Kuldip Singh and Dr. A.S. Anand, JJ. Decided: 18 December 1996.

Background

In 1986 Dr. D.K. Basu, Executive Chairman of Legal Aid Services, West Bengal, wrote to the Chief Justice of India about repeated deaths in police custody and police brutality reported in newspapers. The Court treated the letter as a writ petition and heard it along with similar complaints from other States. Relying on Nilabati Behera v. State of Orissa (1993), the Court held that custodial violence violates Articles 21 and 22 of the Constitution and laid down procedural safeguards for every arrest and detention.

The eleven requirements

  1. Police officers who arrest or interrogate a person must wear accurate, visible identification and name tags with their designations. Their particulars must be recorded in a register.
  2. An arrest memo must be prepared at the time of arrest. It must be attested by at least one witness (a family member or a respectable person of the locality), countersigned by the arrested person, and must state the date and time of arrest.
  3. The arrested person is entitled to have one friend, relative or well-wisher informed of the arrest and the place of detention as soon as practicable.
  4. Where that friend or relative lives outside the district, the time, place of arrest and venue of custody must be notified through the Legal Aid Organisation of the district and the local police station within 8 to 12 hours of the arrest.
  5. The arrested person must be made aware of this right to have someone informed as soon as he or she is arrested or detained.
  6. An entry must be made in the diary at the place of detention recording the arrest, the name of the next friend informed, and the names and particulars of the police officers in whose custody the person is.
  7. On request, the arrested person must be examined at the time of arrest, and any major or minor injuries recorded in an Inspection Memo signed by the arrestee and the arresting officer, with a copy given to the arrestee.
  8. The arrested person must be medically examined by a trained doctor every 48 hours during detention, from a panel of approved doctors.
  9. Copies of all documents, including the arrest memo, must be sent to the Illaqa (area) Magistrate for the record.
  10. The arrested person may be permitted to meet a lawyer during interrogation, though not throughout the interrogation.
  11. A police control room must be set up at every district and State headquarters. Information about the arrest and place of custody must be communicated to it within 12 hours and displayed on a conspicuous notice board.

Consequences of non-compliance

An officer who fails to follow these requirements is liable to departmental action and may also be punished for contempt of court. Every police station must display the guidelines prominently.

Compensation

A victim of custodial violence, or the family of a person who dies in custody, can claim monetary compensation from the State as a public-law remedy under Articles 32 and 226. This remedy is in addition to any civil suit or criminal prosecution against the officers. Sovereign immunity is not a defence to a breach of fundamental rights.
