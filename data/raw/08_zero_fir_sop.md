# Standard Operating Procedure: Zero FIR and e-Zero FIR

*Summary notes on how police stations should register and transfer a Zero FIR under the BNSS, 2023.*

## What is a Zero FIR?

A **Zero FIR** is an FIR for a **cognizable offence** registered at **any police station**, whether or not the offence happened within that station's territorial jurisdiction. It is registered with the serial number **"0"** instead of a regular FIR number. The station with territorial jurisdiction later re-registers it as a regular, numbered FIR.

- The concept was adopted on the recommendation of the **Justice J.S. Verma Committee**, set up after the 2012 Delhi gang rape case.
- Before 2024 it rested on court decisions and government advisories. **Section 173(1) BNSS** now expressly allows information to be given "irrespective of the area where the offence was committed".

**Why it matters:** it avoids delays caused by jurisdiction disputes, helps preserve evidence early, and gets victims timely help, including medical support.

## Duties of the receiving police station

1. **Register immediately.** The station must not refuse to record information merely because the offence occurred outside its area.
2. **Give a free copy** of the FIR to the informant or victim at once.
3. **Arrange medical help** for the victim if needed.
4. **Preserve evidence** and take urgent steps (for example, securing the scene if it is nearby). In cognizable cases the police may arrest the accused, and the accused must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours.
5. **Inform the informant** about the transfer.

## Transfer procedure

### Step 1 – Identify the jurisdiction
Find the police station with territorial jurisdiction over the place of occurrence. Verify carefully, because a Zero FIR can be transferred **only once** through CCTNS (the Crime and Criminal Tracking Network and Systems).

### Step 2 – Transfer the Zero FIR
- Transfer **within 24 hours** of registration.
- Use **CCTNS** if the jurisdictional station is in the same State, or another approved electronic mode if it is in another State.

### Step 3 – Dispatch the physical records
The Officer-in-Charge must send the physical records **immediately and in no case later than 48 hours**. For transfers to another State, the records go through the jurisdictional **Superintendent of Police (SP)** or **Deputy Commissioner of Police (DCP)**.

**Transfer checklist:**
- Signed original complaint and the original Zero FIR
- Statements recorded
- Seizure memos and evidence inventory
- Medical reports (if any)

### Step 4 – Re-registration by the jurisdictional station
On receipt, the jurisdictional police station **immediately assigns a regular FIR number**, continues the investigation from the stage already reached, and makes sure the transfer causes no delay. The victim must be told the new FIR number.

## Time limits at a glance

| Action | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Medical help to the victim | Immediately |
| Free copy of the FIR | Immediately |
| Preserve evidence | Immediately |
| Electronic transfer via CCTNS | Within 24 hours |
| Dispatch of physical records | Within 48 hours |

## e-Zero FIR for cyber financial fraud

For high-value cyber financial frauds, an **e-Zero FIR** system links the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NCRP), the Delhi Police e-FIR system and NCRB's CCTNS. A complaint is converted into a Zero FIR electronically and routed to the jurisdictional cybercrime police station. The victim must **visit the cybercrime police station within three days** to convert it into a regular FIR, in line with the three-day signature rule for e-FIRs under Section 173 BNSS.

## Best practices and supervision

- Maintain a dedicated **Zero FIR register**.
- Train staff regularly.
- Use CCTNS for fast transfer.
- Monitor through Daily Crime Reports; Circle Officers / SDPOs / ACPs should periodically review pending Zero FIR transfers.

## Consequences of refusal

Refusing to register a Zero FIR for a cognizable offence goes against the Supreme Court's directions in **Lalita Kumari v. Govt. of U.P.** and can expose the officer to action, including under **Section 199 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)**. The complainant can use the remedy in **Section 173(4) BNSS**: write to the SP, then approach the Magistrate.
